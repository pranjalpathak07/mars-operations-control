About this prototype

Built for the Texas McCombs Operations Fellows Mars strategy exercise

This tool turns a broad colonization question into a transparent operating model. It focuses on capacity, bottlenecks, buffers, resilience, and phased growth. The numbers are planning assumptions for decision testing. They are not a validated engineering or NASA mission model.

AI use: AI helped structure the operating variables, challenge assumptions, and build the simulator. Scenario testing was then used to refine the recommendation logic.

How to use this Mars colony simulator

What is this website for?

Imagine you manage a small colony on Mars. Every person needs water, food, and power every day. Some supplies are produced on Mars. Others arrive from Earth. This simulator helps you test whether the colony can support its population, survive a delay or failure, and safely grow.

You do not need technical knowledge. Change one input, then watch the results update immediately.

Use it in four simple steps

  1. Choose a scenario.

    Start with Normal operations. Then try a delayed delivery, a failed production system, or a larger population.

  2. Change the operating inputs.

    Move the sliders for population, water, food, and power. You can also change the inventory buffer and time between Earth deliveries.

  3. Read the live results.

    Colony outlook shows the readiness score, first bottleneck, days to shortage, power use, and reliance on Earth.

  4. Follow the phase recommendation.

    Expand means there is enough headroom. Hold means improve the weakest area before growing. Critical means stabilize the colony immediately.

How to read the resource bars

Dark orange: resource produced on Mars

Light orange: resource supplied by Earth

White line: total demand that must be met

The percentage on the right compares available supply with demand. Above 100% means supply covers demand. Below 100% means there is a deficit. The sentence below each bar states the margin or shortage.

What should you try first?

Use Normal operations as your baseline. Next, click Resupply delayed and see which resource runs out first. Then select System failure and test water, food, or power. Finally, increase the population until the recommendation changes.

The model is a simple decision tool for comparing operating choices. It is not a scientifically validated Mars mission plan.

Transparent model

Model assumptions and formulas

Planning basis

  • Base demand is normalized to a 20-person colony.
  • Water starts with full local production plus a recycle allowance.
  • Food uses a mix of local production and scheduled Earth supply.
  • Power demand includes a 10% operating reserve.
  • Inventory protects deficits until the next resupply arrives.

Core formulas

Demand
Base demand × population ÷ 20
Safety margin
(available capacity − demand) ÷ demand
Days to shortage
usable inventory ÷ daily resource deficit
Earth dependency
Earth-supplied critical demand ÷ total critical demand

Decision thresholds

Expand
Readiness 80+, all margins at least 15%, runway covers the resupply interval.
Hold
Readiness 55–79 or a margin is below 15%.
Critical
Readiness below 55, capacity below demand, or runway is shorter than the active delay.

This is an operations simulation for an MBA fellowship application. It is not an engineering certification model.

Readiness score

Readiness combines four explainable components: capacity coverage (45%), inventory runway (25%), power resilience (20%), and low Earth dependency (10%). Each component is capped at 100 before weighting.
