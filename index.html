About this prototype

Built for the Texas McCombs Operations Fellows Mars strategy exercise

This tool turns a broad colonization question into a transparent operating model. It focuses on capacity, bottlenecks, buffers, resilience, and phased growth. The numbers are planning assumptions for decision testing. They are not a validated engineering or NASA mission model.

AI use: AI helped structure the operating variables, challenge assumptions, and build the simulator. Scenario testing was then used to refine the recommendation logic.

Simple walkthrough

How this website works

No Mars or operations knowledge needed

You are the manager of a Mars colony. Your job is to keep everyone supplied with water, food, and power.

This website is a simple “what if?” tool. You change the colony plan. The website shows what may go wrong first and what you should do next.

Simple example: If 20 people need 100 units of food, but the colony only has 80 units available, food is short by 20%. Food becomes the bottleneck.

Section 2

How to use it in four steps

  1. Choose a situation to test.

    Click one of the four scenario tabs. Begin with Normal operations.

  2. Change one assumption.

    Move a slider. For example, raise the population from 20 to 35 people.

  3. Watch the results change.

    The numbers and resource bars update immediately. No Calculate button is needed.

  4. Read the recommendation.

    The bottom panel tells you whether to expand, hold, or stabilize, and explains why.

Section 3

What each control means

Colony population

How many people live on Mars. More people increase water, food, and power demand.

Try: 20 → 40 people
Water system capacity

How much water the colony can extract and recycle compared with the original plan.

100% = planned output
Food system capacity

How much food is grown locally. Any missing food may need to come from Earth.

55% = 55 of every 100 units
Power capacity

How much electricity the colony can produce. The model also keeps a small operating reserve.

Higher capacity = more headroom
Inventory buffer

Emergency supplies already stored on Mars. A larger buffer gives the colony more time during trouble.

75 days = 75 days at full demand
Resupply interval

How often supplies arrive from Earth. Longer intervals mean the colony must survive longer on its own.

120 days = one delivery every 120 days

Section 4

What the four scenarios mean

01
Normal operations

Everything works as planned. Use this as your starting point.

02
Resupply delayed

The next Earth delivery does not arrive on time. The model removes that delivery and checks how long stored supplies last.

03
System failure

Water, food, or power output suddenly drops. Choose the failed system and how much output remains.

04
Population expansion

The colony grows to 45 people. This quickly tests whether today’s systems can support a larger population.

Section 5

How to read the resource bars

Dark orange: resource produced on Mars

Light orange: resource supplied by Earth

White line: total demand that must be met

112%Supply is 12% above demandEnough
100%Supply exactly matches demandNo cushion
62%Supply covers only 62% of demand38% short

The resource with the smallest margin is the bottleneck. It is the first resource you should improve.

Section 6

How to understand the results

Readiness score

A quick 0–100 summary. Higher is better. It combines capacity, inventory, power resilience, and reliance on Earth.

First bottleneck

The weakest resource. Fix this first because it limits the whole colony.

Days to shortage

How many days remain before stored supplies run out when there is a daily deficit.

Power utilization

How much of power capacity is being used. More than 100% means demand is greater than supply.

Earth dependency

The share of critical daily needs that still relies on deliveries from Earth.

Recommendation

The action suggested by the model: Expand, Hold, or Critical.

Transparent model

Model assumptions and formulas

Planning basis

  • Base demand is normalized to a 20-person colony.
  • Water starts with full local production plus a recycle allowance.
  • Food uses a mix of local production and scheduled Earth supply.
  • Power demand includes a 10% operating reserve.
  • Inventory protects deficits until the next resupply arrives.

Core formulas

Demand
Base demand × population ÷ 20
Safety margin
(available capacity − demand) ÷ demand
Days to shortage
usable inventory ÷ daily resource deficit
Earth dependency
Earth-supplied critical demand ÷ total critical demand

Decision thresholds

Expand
Readiness 80+, all margins at least 15%, runway covers the resupply interval.
Hold
Readiness 55–79 or a margin is below 15%.
Critical
Readiness below 55, capacity below demand, or runway is shorter than the active delay.

This is an operations simulation for an MBA fellowship application. It is not an engineering certification model.

Readiness score

Readiness combines four explainable components: capacity coverage (45%), inventory runway (25%), power resilience (20%), and low Earth dependency (10%). Each component is capped at 100 before weighting.
